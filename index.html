01 The Two Camps Strategic Convergence

The map has folded. Two coalitions now finance, arm,
and underwrite nearly every war on earth.

No bloc is formally signed. Both sides bicker internally and trade with each other across embargoes. But behind almost every active front lies a tangle of supply lines, missile transfers, and diplomatic vetoes that resolves into the same two patterns of patronage.

```

The Western Bloc

G7 · NATO · QUAD · AUKUS · Coalition of the Willing

Anchored by the United States and the European democracies, the Western coalition controls the dollar reserve system, the SWIFT rail, the IMF and World Bank, and roughly half of global GDP from a tenth of its population. Through the France– and UK–led Coalition of the Willing, it underwrites Ukraine; through US strikes and Israeli operations it confronts Iran; and through critical-mineral deals it is now contesting eastern Congo and the Sahel.

The CRINK Axis

"Axis of Upheaval" · Adversary Entente · BRICS+ Penumbra

Not a treaty alliance — a strategic convergence of four revisionist states united against US-led order. Russia and China anchor it; Iran and North Korea operate as the kinetic edge. Pyongyang has shipped millions of artillery shells and troops to Ukraine; Tehran sent the drones; Beijing supplies the dual-use components and the discounted oil that keeps Russia liquid. Around them sits a wider BRICS+ of neutrals quietly building parallel financial rails.

03 The Silent Wars No Bodies, but Bigger Stakes

The wars fought
with tariffs, code, capital, and quiet money.

For every kinetic theater, there are five economic ones. The same coalitions that arm Ukraine and Iran are also fighting through customs codes, chip export licences, payment rails, and influence campaigns from Caracas to Kinshasa.

```
§
Front 01 · Tariffs & Trade

The tariff war.

Largest US tax increase as % of GDP since 1993. Avg cost: $1,500/household in 2026.

The Trump administration has remade global commerce. A 10% across-the-board surcharge under Section 122 took effect 25 February 2026; Section 232 chips, Section 301 China escalations and a 100% EV tariff stack on top. Beijing responded with rare-earth export controls — gallium, germanium, antimony — that hit Western chipmakers hardest.

Section 301 — China
25% on $250B industrial goods · 15% on $112B consumer goods · 100% on Chinese EVs
Section 232 — Semiconductors
Took effect Jan 2026. 25% on AI chips bound for re-export. Section 232 steel/aluminum still in force at 25%.
Section 122 — Across-the-Board
10% import surcharge effective 25 Feb 2026, applied to ~$1.2T of imports. Trump signaling 15%.
China Counter-Volley
Export controls on gallium, germanium, antimony. 7nm chip via SMIC/Huawei. Memory hoarding. Critical-mineral squeeze.
New Section 301 Probes
11 March 2026: 16 economies under "structural excess capacity" investigation, including EU, Japan, Korea, India.
Russia · Iran
Column-2 duty rates expanded on Russian metals, energy, chemicals. JCPOA snapback restored full Iran sanctions Sept 2025.

Front 02 · Technology

The chip war.

"Bifurcated development paths" — semis market projected to shrink up to 34% by 2026 if tariffs hold above 100%.

The deepest structural cleavage. Both sides now pursue parallel tech stacks. Washington uses BIS export controls and CHIPS Act subsidies; Beijing answers with state funds, equipment indigenisation, and rare-earth leverage. AI chips are the focal point — and the Trump administration is now charging US firms a 25% revenue share for the privilege of selling them to China.

AI Accelerators
Nvidia H200 + AMD MI325X exports to China permitted with 25% revenue cut to US Treasury. House AI OVERWATCH bill in markup.
Equipment & Foundries
ASML, AMAT, TEL controls. TSMC US fab build-out; Samsung NAND moved from China to US to harvest CHIPS Act money.
Chinese Indigenisation
Huawei's stealth fabs, SMIC 7nm, "10nm chip tool" announced Q1 2026. Memory hoarding by Chinese leaders amid trade war.
Critical Minerals
China holds dominant share of refined gallium, germanium, antimony, processed rare earths. South Korean chip supply chains hit by export curbs.
Data & Cloud
Quiet front: cross-border data localization rules accelerating. >50% of industrial manufacturers expanding edge compute within 18 months.
Sub-fronts
Quantum, biotech, EVs, batteries, drones, AI models, undersea cable disputes — all now dual-use, all sanctioned at the margins.

Front 03 · Capital & Currency

The dedollar war.

Central banks added 1,045 tonnes of gold in 2024 — third straight 1,000+ tonne year.

The slowest-moving but most strategically consequential battlefield. SWIFT exclusion of Russian banks turned theoretical alternatives into operational ones. China's CIPS now has 1,500+ participants. The BRICS Unit launched a 100-unit pilot on 31 October 2025 — 40% gold, 60% BRICS currencies. 90% of Russia–China trade now settles in local currency.

Sanctions Architecture
16,000+ measures on Russia. Iran JCPOA snapback restored full UN sanctions Sept 2025. Secondary sanctions chase Chinese banks.
Alt-Rails
CIPS (China) · SPFS (Russia) · UPI (India) · BRICS Pay messaging layer interlinks them — used to bypass SWIFT.
BRICS Unit
Settlement instrument backed 40% gold / 60% BRICS currencies. Pilot launched Oct 2025. Designed for trade, not retail.
CBDC Interoperability
Digital ruble · digital yuan (e-CNY in Guangdong) · digital rupee — interlink protocols under live development among BRICS-3.
New Development Bank
$30B disbursed in 2024; ~⅓ in local currencies. India assumed BRICS chair Jan 2026; New Delhi summit due Aug/Sept.
Gold
Central-bank gold buying broke records three years running. Russia, China, India, Türkiye, Poland leading. Bretton Woods III talk back.

Front 04 · Economic Coercion

The commodity war.

"Weaponizing dependencies" cuts both ways — and almost always asymmetrically.

The line between trade policy and warfare has dissolved. Critical inputs — energy, food, minerals, fertilizer — are now tools of coercion. Russia weaponises grain corridors and gas; China weaponises rare earths; the West weaponises the dollar and reinsurance.

Energy
EU off Russian pipeline gas (largely). Russia oil to India + China at discount. China imports Russian oil up 80% vs 2021; Iranian oil to China up 400%.
Food & Fertilizer
Black Sea grain weaponised; Russia withdrew from grain deal. Sahel famines; Sudan and Gaza famines declared.
Critical Minerals
Cobalt, copper, lithium battles in DRC, Zambia, Indonesia. KoBold and Virtus (US) vs Chinese state miners. Indonesia's nickel ban set the model.
Shipping & Insurance
Houthi attacks rerouted Asia–Europe shipping around the Cape; insurance premia tripled. Sanctions-driven "shadow fleet" of tankers now > 1,000 vessels.

Front 05 · Geopolitical Influence

The influence war.

Where the two coalitions buy ports, mines, votes, narratives — and occasionally each other.

Beneath every kinetic and economic front sits a slower, broader contest: for ports and bases, for UN votes, for telecom infrastructure, for the political coloration of mid-sized powers. The US still leads in alliances and finance; China leads in infrastructure footprint; Russia operates as the spoiler-of-choice; the Gulf states now act as autonomous mid-powers in their own right.

Editorial Notes

A war does not have to be declared to be a war. In 2026, much of the world's conflict is undeclared — fought in tariff schedules, export-licence applications, central-bank gold vaults, and the back rooms where mining concessions are signed.

This dossier compresses an intentionally wide field. Coalitions are not treaty alliances; many states pictured on one side of a chip war (e.g. India, UAE) sit on the other side of a financial-rail war. Read the lines, not the flags.
